# LUMEN Germany — launch recommendation

## Recommendation

Launch at **€2.19 per unit** with an **accessible-premium** position: clearly above mass/heritage offers, but below the boutique-adaptogenic ceiling. Start with **DTC Online + Gym & Office in Berlin**, then expand to Munich and selectively add grocery once repeat purchase and acquisition economics are demonstrated.

## Why €2.19

The supplied price test shows a meaningful trade-off:

- €1.79: 61.7% estimated acceptance, but only €0.40 contribution/unit in grocery and €0.77–€0.81 in DTC/Gym.
- €2.19: 51.7% acceptance, with €0.63 grocery, €1.16 DTC and €1.13 Gym & Office contribution/unit.
- €2.59: contribution rises further, but acceptance falls to 26.7%.

€2.19 therefore protects much more unit economics than €1.79 while avoiding the severe acceptance loss at €2.59. It also sits inside VoltFit's premium-performance band (€2.1–€2.7) while remaining below Root & Rise's boutique band (€2.5–€3.1).

## Channel sequence

DTC and Gym & Office are the preferred proof-of-concept channels because the supplied channel economics leave LUMEN with substantially more contribution per unit than grocery. Grocery is strategically important for eventual reach, but should not be the only launch channel while CAC/payback is a core CFO concern.

A practical initial mix to test is **40% DTC / 20% Gym & Office / 40% Grocery**. At €2.19, the price-test contribution figures imply a weighted contribution of about **€0.95 per unit**. The website lets the team change this mix rather than treating it as fixed.

## Geography and timing

Berlin is the first city: Exhibit 1 assigns it 18% of the illustrative German market and 9% regional CAGR. Munich follows at 15% and 9%. Both have the strongest growth assumption in the supplied city split. The next iteration should combine Exhibit 12's seasonality index with launch lead times to select the launch month explicitly.

## What this choice deliberately does not optimise

This recommendation does **not** maximise immediate survey acceptance (that would favour €1.79), and it does **not** maximise contribution per accepted unit (that would favour €2.59). It optimises for a middle path: credible premium positioning with enough acceptance to learn quickly, while preserving stronger unit economics for CAC payback.

## Data handling

There is no German LUMEN sales history. The recommendation triangulates German survey/market/competitor evidence with LUMEN's existing-market economics rather than presenting a German sales forecast as observed fact. Customer name/email-style fields in `customer_survey.csv` are not used or exposed in the prototype.
